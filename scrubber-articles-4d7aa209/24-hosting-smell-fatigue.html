Hosting this season? Why your nose can't tell what your guests will smell

Your nose stops noticing your home's smell within minutes. Your guests' noses don't. A practical prep list for hosting, and where an air scrubber fits.

By the Jaspr team[Publish date]5 min read
Offer bar Sits here only if the client approves a current offer.

You smell every other home the moment you walk in. Not your own.

Every home has a smell, and yours does too. Last night’s dinner, the dog’s bed, the damp towel on the hook. You stopped noticing it within minutes of moving in, which is exactly how noses are meant to work. Your guests’ noses haven’t had that time. They walk in and smell your home exactly as it is.

That’s worth knowing before a season of dinners, drop-ins and relatives with overnight bags. The good news: most of what guests notice comes from a short list of sources, and most of the fixes are free. Open a window. Empty a bin. Wash a blanket.

This piece is that list, in the order we’d do it. We’ll also be straight about where an air scrubber fits. It helps. It isn’t a magic trick, and it doesn’t replace the bin, the open window or a good wash of the dog’s blanket.

Your nose clocked out a long time ago

Smell fatigue is real, and it’s ordinary. When the same scent reaches your nose all day, your brain turns the volume down on it . That’s why you can’t smell your own house, your own pet or your own cooking, and why a stranger can pick up all three from the front step.

It cuts both ways. The pleasant smells fade too, so the candle you lit at noon is barely there by dinner. Your nose isn’t broken. It’s doing its job. It just isn’t a reliable test of your home.

There is a free test, though. Leave the house for a few minutes, then walk back in and pay attention to the first breath in the front hall. That’s the closest you’ll get to a guest’s nose. Better still, ask a friend who doesn’t live there.

Air freshener is air makeup

A spray, a plug-in or a candle adds a scent to the air. It doesn’t take anything out. Underneath, the smell you were trying to cover is still there, and now it has company. Some guests notice the layering before they notice either smell.

Candles add something else, too. Burning one releases fine particles into the room along with the fragrance . One candle in a room with fresh air is a nice touch. Five in a closed room is a lot.

A better order: find the source, remove it, move some air, and then, if you still want it, add a scent.

Start with the sources

Smells come from things, so go find the things. Do these the day before, while there’s still time to air the house out:

  • Trash, recycling and compost. Empty them, then rinse the bins. The can itself holds smell.
  • The litter box. Scoop it, and move it somewhere guests won’t pass on the way to the bathroom.
  • Pet beds, throws and the dog’s favorite blanket. Wash them. Soft things hold smell and give it back slowly.
  • Dishwasher and sink. Run a full cycle, empty it, and flush the drain with hot water.
  • The fridge. Find the one container that has quietly turned.
  • The bathroom. Run the fan, swap the towels, wipe around the base of the toilet.
  • Shoes by the door. Shoes carry whatever they’ve walked through. Give them a spot off the main path.

None of it is glamorous. All of it beats a plug-in.

Let some air through before they arrive

Fresh air is still the cheapest move you have. While the house is empty, open windows on opposite sides for ten or fifteen minutes, so air actually crosses through instead of sitting in the corners. Do it a couple of hours before the first guest, not as the doorbell rings.

If it’s cold out, short and wide beats long and cracked. A few minutes with several windows open swaps the air without chilling the walls and furniture .

While you cook, switch the range hood on before the pan is hot and open a window nearby. Plan the smelly part of the menu early and the finishing touches late, so the kitchen has time to clear before people ask what’s for dinner.

Where an air scrubber fits

A scrubber works on the air that’s already in the room. Carbon is the layer for smell. It holds onto odor molecules as air passes through . A particle filter does nothing for odor, and carbon does nothing for lint, which is why the cartridge has both. Neither one fixes a full trash can. They work on what’s already in the air, not on what’s still giving it off.

How many, and where

Jaspr is sized for a room, not a whole house. The site’s guidance is one in each bedroom, one in the living or cooking area, and one in a home office or gym. For hosting, start in the room where people will actually gather, which is usually the one next to the kitchen. Overnight guests? The guest room is a room too.

Leave some clearance around the base so the intake can reach the room’s air. One unit won’t change a whole house, and we won’t pretend it does.

A short list before the doorbell rings

Walk out, walk back in, and trust the first breath of your front hall over your own nose. Remove the sources. Open the windows while the house is empty. Cook with the hood on. Then add filtration for what’s still hanging in the air.
