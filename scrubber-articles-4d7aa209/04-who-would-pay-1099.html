Who would pay $1,099 for an air scrubber? What the price actually buys

The $1,099 Jaspr Air Scrubber, part by part: the unit, $398 a year in filters, the warranty, the 30-day buy-back and who should skip it.

By the Jaspr team[Publish date]4 min read

The sticker says $1,099. The price you’ll really pay shows up over five years.

You probably have a cheaper unit open in another tab, and some cheaper units move as much air at top speed. So $1,099 for a tower that sits in the corner of a room is a fair thing to question. Who pays this, and for what?

This article takes the price apart: the machine, the yearly filter, the warranty and the 30-day buy-back that lets you change your mind. It says what each part is, what it isn’t and who should skip it. Prices come from jaspr.co and the makers’ own pages in October 2026, and they move.

The machine: $1,099

A tall steel tower of about 25 lb, in sand or white, with a green light ring and a live air-quality display that has a dark mode. Plug it in and it runs, with no app. A grate circling the whole base draws air in from every direction. Shipping is free, and the first filter comes in the box. A built-in sensor raises the fan on its own when the air changes. Owners mention cooking, hairspray and toast smoke, and some report it reading orange or red near a humidifier.

"Air scrubber" is the restoration trade’s word for this kind of machine. Jaspr’s founder, Michael Feldstein, was a disaster-restoration contractor before he built one for the home.

Here is what it isn’t. It isn’t the most air per dollar. Jaspr lists 340 CFM smoke CADR at top speed and 114 CFM at speed 1, where it runs at 33 dBA. A $199.99 Winix 5510 lists 253 CFM at its top. You pay for the build and for airflow that stays quiet, not for the biggest number.

The yearly part: $398

The filter is one cartridge: pre-filter, particle filter and 1 lb of carbon, swapped whole every six months in under 30 seconds, with no vacuuming. It costs $199 each time on the filter subscription, or $299 one-time. That’s $398 a year, or $598 without the subscription. On the subscription it works out to $1.09 a day.

Candidly, that’s high. The rivals we checked run roughly $50 to $350 a year in filters: $49.99 for a Winix 5510, $89 for a Coway Airmega 250, $171.99 for an AirDoctor 3500. Jaspr also publishes no filtration grade. The $199 buys the cartridge, the lifetime warranty, free two-way shipping, reminder emails a week, five days and two days before each shipment, and pause or cancel any time.

What the warranty and the buy-back change

Jaspr’s warranty is for life, with a replacement unit and free two-way shipping, while you stay on the filter subscription and change the filter every six months. Most makers we checked list one to two years on the unit. Some extend to five or ten, with conditions of their own.

The condition is the catch. The subscription keeps the warranty alive, so the real price of the lifetime promise is the filter bill. Put the two together: $1,099 plus five years of filters at $398 is about $3,089, or about $51 a month. The first filter is in the box, so the real figure is a little lower.

The 30-day buy-back is separate. If you decide within 30 days that Jaspr isn’t for you, it buys the unit back at the full price you paid, and asks you to hold on to the box. It’s a decision window, not a promise about what any room will measure, and it ends before the first filter change.

What has been tested

Independent proof is narrow: one lab report on airborne mold. LMS Technologies, a microbiology lab in Bloomington, Minnesota, released Aspergillus niger into a 4,000 cubic foot chamber at 71°F and 45% humidity and compared the unit against a run with it off. Corrected removal reached 97.1% at 60 minutes and 99.8% at 120 minutes. The report doesn’t state the fan speed.

A lab chamber isn’t your living room; results depend on the room, the mold and how it’s measured. Air purifiers are not a substitute for mold remediation.

What the price doesn’t buy

  • The most air per dollar. A cheaper unit can match Jaspr at top speed.
  • The lowest filter bill. Several units cost a fraction to run.
  • A published filtration grade. Jaspr publishes none.
  • A whole home. One unit serves one room. Jaspr recommends one per bedroom, one in the living or cooking area and one in an office or gym. Three units are $3,297, plus about $1,194 a year in filters.

Who it is for, and who it isn’t

Jaspr suits someone who wants one machine to run quietly all day in the room they use most, built like furniture and meant to last, and who would rather pay up front than shop for a cheaper replacement later. It also suits anyone who wants 30 days to test it in their own room. Two owners put it in their own words:

"These are so worth the money! … It’s now a game to see who can cook without making the living room Jaspr go crazy." Angela O.
"… it’s also a teaching tool. I’ve made several changes in my household thanks to the sensors at work in the Jaspr." Donnie G.

Those are two opinions, not results. Jaspr doesn’t suit someone who wants the lowest running cost, the most airflow per dollar or a named filtration grade, or who would rather not run a subscription. For them, a cheaper unit is the better choice.

The short version

$1,099 buys a steel machine that is quiet at a speed that moves air, a lifetime warranty if you keep the filter schedule and 30 days to change your mind. It doesn’t buy the most air or the cheapest filters. If the first list sounds like you, try one.
