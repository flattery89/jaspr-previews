Proof and seasons

New year, new air: start the year with cleaner air in the room you spend the most time in

A simple January reset for the air at home: start with one room, place the unit well, put the filter date on the calendar, and check it with numbers.

By the Jaspr team[Publish date]4 min read

You reset the closet and the pantry every January. The air never makes the list.

By mid-January the junk drawer is sorted, the inbox is down to zero and the calendar is clean. The air in the room you’re standing in hasn’t had a second thought. There’s nothing to see, so it never gets a turn.

There’s a second reason it slips: your nose adjusts. Your nose stops registering a smell within minutes. Your guests’ noses don’t . So this isn’t a promise about how you’ll feel. It’s a plan for one afternoon, checked with numbers: one room, a placement, a filter date and a short list.

Start with one room, not the whole house

Jaspr is sized for a room, not a house. The site suggests one per bedroom, one in the living or cooking area, and one in a home office or gym. For a first Jaspr, pick the room where you spend the most hours. For most people that’s the bedroom. For some it’s a home office.

Measure it once: length × width × ceiling height. A 12 by 14 foot bedroom with an 8 foot ceiling is 1,344 cubic feet. Air changes per hour = CFM × 60 ÷ room volume. At fan speed 1, Jaspr moves 114 CFM (smoke CADR) at 33 dBA, which is about 5.1 air changes an hour in that room. At the top speed, 340 CFM, it’s about 15.

A bigger room changes the numbers. A 250 square foot living room with an 8 foot ceiling is 2,000 cubic feet, and the same two speeds give about 3.4 and 10.2 air changes an hour. These are yardsticks, not guarantees: doors, furniture and where the unit sits all change how well a room mixes.

Place it where air can reach it

Jaspr’s intake is a grate that circles the whole base, so it draws air in from every direction. Give it open floor space on all sides rather than pushing it flat into a corner or behind a sofa . Plug it in and it starts working. No app required.

One placement note, because owners keep bringing it up: if you run a humidifier, keep it as far from the unit as the room allows. Owners report the sensor reading orange or red near one.

Put the filter date on the calendar

The filter is the one thing every air cleaner asks of you. Jaspr’s is a single cartridge, swapped whole every six months in under 30 seconds. No vacuuming. On the subscription a new one ships every six months, with reminders before each shipment, and the lifetime warranty stays in force while you keep to that schedule. If your Jaspr arrives in November, the next filter is due around May.

If you own a different air cleaner, look up its filter schedule today and write the date down. That one habit matters whatever brand you own.

Notice what sets it off, and what you cover up

The sensor makes the invisible visible, and owners learn from it. Margaret F. (May 5, 2026) noticed that when she cooks, "despite using an overhead exhaust, the Jaspr immediately begins to scrub the air." Corinne G. (January 6, 2025) wrote that it "kicks in as soon as we start cooking and reminds us to use our kitchen fan."

Donnie G. (December 13, 2025) says he’s "made several changes in my household thanks to the sensors at work in the Jaspr," and that he got rid of his scented candles. Treat a reading as a prompt, not proof. The unit’s own screen isn’t evidence of anything. If you like numbers, take a reading with a separate monitor before and after .

Candles, sprays and plug-in fresheners hide a smell. They don’t remove what’s making it. You don’t have to give them up, but try a week without them in the room you’re starting with. Then ask a friend to walk in and tell you what the room smells like. Your nose won’t be able to.

What to expect in the first week

Plug it in and the first thing you’ll probably see is a green reading. Some owners read that as a sign the machine wasn’t doing anything. A green screen isn’t proof of anything either way. What you can check in week one is smaller and more useful: how loud your chosen speed is, what makes the fan ramp up, and whether the intake is clear. What you can check at six months is the filter. And what a lab can check is in the test report, which states its conditions.

A simple January checklist

  • Pick the room where you spend the most hours.
  • Measure it, then work out air changes with CFM × 60 ÷ room volume.
  • Find an open spot with room around the base, and plug it in.
  • Write the filter date on the calendar, six months out.
  • Run the kitchen fan when you cook, and keep a humidifier away from the unit.
  • Check back in a month: what makes the fan speed up, and is the speed you chose one you can live with?

One room is enough to start

You don’t need to reset the whole house. You need one room, one date and a few numbers you’ve checked yourself.
