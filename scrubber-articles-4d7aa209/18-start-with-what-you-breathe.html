Start with what you breathe: why cleaner air at home comes first

You can skip a product. You can't skip a breath. Why Jaspr starts with the air in your home, and what an air scrubber does about it.

By the Jaspr team[Publish date]5 min read
Offer bar Sits here only if the client approves a current offer.

You read the label on the soap. The air in your home doesn’t come with one.

Most of us have spent the last few years trimming the household list. Shorter ingredient labels. A harder look at what lands on the kids’ plates. Each of those is a choice, made one purchase at a time. Air isn’t. You take it in every minute, chosen or not. A room can be spotless, smell like lemon, and still tell you very little about what’s moving through it.

That’s why we think a cleaner home starts with the air. Not because it’s frightening. Because it’s constant, you can’t see it, and you can measure it. Here is where Jaspr’s thinking comes from, what an air scrubber does about it, and where we’d tell you to stay skeptical.

It started on job sites, not in a lab

Mike Feldstein, Jaspr’s founder, spent years as a disaster-restoration contractor. His work at Fort McMurray in 2016 covered about 150 restored homes. People in that trade learn quickly that a room that looks finished and a room that is finished aren’t always the same thing.

He also watched consumer air purifiers fail on job sites. They weren’t built for the work. So he built a machine that was.

Restoration crews have a name for that machine: an air scrubber. It’s the reason Jaspr is called one. It’s the job-site idea, made quiet and handsome enough to stand in a bedroom.

A room can look clean and still have things floating in it

The dust you wipe off a shelf is the part that has already settled. Finer particles can stay in a room’s air long after the surfaces look clean. Cooking, candles, a dog bed, the fragrance plug-in by the stairs: each puts something into the air, and you won’t see most of it.

Your nose makes it harder. It stops registering a smell within minutes, so the home you live in smells like nothing to you. Your guests’ noses don’t adapt that way. They walk in fresh.

So we don’t trust "looks fine" or "smells fine." Neither is a measurement. A measurement is a number from an instrument, with a room and a time attached. We come back to that below.

Air is the one input you can’t skip

We’re not going to tell you what any particle does to a body. That isn’t our claim. Our claim is simpler. Air is the thing you take in all day, in every room, and it’s the one input most of us have never looked at.

Food comes with a label. Water can be tested. You can change what you buy at the store. You can’t change that you breathe. So if you’re deciding where a cleaner home begins, begin with the thing that never stops.

Start with the source, then add a machine

No machine fixes a source. If something is putting particles or odors into a room, such as a burning candle, an aerosol spray or a stovetop with no fan, the most effective move is usually to remove or reduce it, then bring in outdoor air. That’s the order the EPA gives: source control, ventilation, then air cleaning.

What’s left is where a machine earns its place. A scrubber works on the particles still moving through the room after you’ve done the sensible things, or when you can’t open a window, or while dinner’s on the stove. It’s the third step. It’s not a replacement for the first two.

What a Jaspr air scrubber does about the rest

Jaspr is a tall steel cylinder with a grate that circles the whole base, so it draws room air in from every direction at once. The air passes through one cartridge: a pre-filter, a particle filter and 1 lb of carbon for odors. That intake matters because a filter only cleans the air that moves through it.

At its lowest fan speed a Jaspr moves 114 CFM (smoke CADR) at 33 dBA. In a 2,000 cubic foot room, which is 250 square feet with an 8-foot ceiling, that’s about 3.4 air changes an hour. At top speed it’s 340 CFM, up to 58 dBA, and about 10.2 changes an hour. Those are yardsticks, not guarantees. Furniture, doors and where the unit sits change how well a room mixes.

The built-in sensor ramps the fan up on its own when the air changes. Owners tell us they see it happen with cooking, hairspray and toast smoke. There’s no app to download, and it makes zero ozone.

Proof is a number from an instrument, not a screen

A Jaspr has a live air-quality display. It’s a convenience. It isn’t the proof, and it can read "Excellent" on the day you plug it in.

Proof is a reading from a standalone particle counter, with the room and the time on it, or the old cartridge in your hand at six months, from one home’s air. Yours will look different. When we quote a reading, we’ll name the instrument it came from.
