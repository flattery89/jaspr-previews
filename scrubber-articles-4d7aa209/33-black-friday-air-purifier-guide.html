Black Friday air purifier guide: what to look for before you buy

A buyer's guide to holiday air purifier sales: airflow at your speed, noise, filter cost per year, warranty, returns and the sale-price trap.

By the Jaspr team[Publish date]5 min read
Offer bar Sits here only if the client approves a current offer.

The countdown timer is loud. The year-two filter bill is quiet.

It’s Black Friday week and the air purifiers are marked down. A big percent off, a big number on the box, a timer counting down in red. You can see all of it. What you can’t see is whether the machine will do its job in your room, or what the filters will cost once the sale is over.

So compare six things instead: airflow at the speed you’ll run, noise at that speed, filter cost per year, warranty and returns, who tested it, and what the discount is really off. It takes about ten minutes. We’ll use Jaspr’s own numbers as the example, because we can vouch for them, and say where they’re weaker than a rival’s.

1. Airflow at the speed you’ll actually run

Airflow is how much air a unit moves a minute, usually shown as CADR or CFM. Many spec sheets lead with the top speed, which is rarely the one you’ll live with. A square-foot rating is the same number divided by an assumed ceiling height and air-change rate, and brands pick different ones. Compare the airflow, not the square feet.

Do the sum yourself: air changes per hour = CFM × 60 ÷ room volume in cubic feet. Jaspr moves 114 CFM (smoke CADR) at fan speed 1 and 340 CFM at its top speed. In a 2,000 cubic foot room, a 250 square foot room with an 8 foot ceiling, that’s about 3.4 air changes an hour at speed 1 and about 10.2 at the top. A yardstick, not a guarantee. And at top speed, some cheaper units move as much or more air: one $200 unit we checked lists 253 CFM, and a $659 one lists 340 .

2. Noise at that same speed

A machine that’s loud at the speed you need ends up turned down or switched off. Ask for dBA at each speed and the distance it was measured from. A top-speed figure tells you the loudest it gets, not what you’ll hear day to day. Decibels are logarithmic, and a common rule of thumb is that every 10 dBA sounds about twice as loud .

Jaspr runs at 33 dBA on its lowest speed and up to 58 dBA at the top . The fan can also ramp up by itself when the sensor picks up cooking or hairspray, so you’ll hear it rise, then settle.

3. What the filters cost per year

This is the number sale prices hide. Five-year cost = unit price + (filter cost per year × 5). Run that sum on every unit on your list. For Jaspr it’s $1,099 + ($398 × 5) = $3,089 . Filters are $199 each on the subscription, $398 a year, or $299 each bought one at a time, $598 a year.

That’s more than many rivals charge. The units we checked run roughly $50 to $350 a year in filters . The $199 includes a full cartridge (pre-filter, particle filter and 1 lb of carbon), free shipping, reminders before each shipment, and pause or cancel any time. It also keeps the lifetime warranty in force. If the lowest running cost is your first priority, another unit may suit you better.

4. The warranty and the return window, with their conditions

Read the conditions, not the headline. Many warranties depend on something you have to keep doing, such as buying filters. Ask how long it lasts, what it covers, who pays shipping, and whether a sale purchase changes any of it. Do the same for returns: how many days, counted from when, and whether you pay to send it back.

Jaspr’s return is a 30-day buy-back at the full price you paid; keep the box . The warranty is for life, with free two-way shipping and a replacement unit, while you’re enrolled in the filter subscription and replacing the filter every six months. Stop, and the lifetime coverage stops with it .

5. Proof: who tested it, and on what

Look for a test that names the lab, the conditions and what was measured. Then look at what the maker doesn’t publish. Jaspr publishes no filtration grade, and we won’t imply one. Some makers do publish one, and if that matters to you, weigh it.

What Jaspr has is a lab report you can read. In February 2025 LMS Technologies, an independent microbiology lab, tested a Jaspr against airborne Aspergillus niger in a 4,000 cubic foot chamber at 71°F and 45% humidity, compared with the device off. It removed 97.1% of airborne mold spores at 60 minutes and 99.8% at 120. A lab chamber isn’t your living room; results depend on the room, the mold and how it’s measured. Air purifiers are not a substitute for mold remediation.

6. The sale-price trap

A discount is worth what the old price was worth. Ask three questions of any sale: what did it cost before, does the discount reach the filters, and is the return window or the warranty different on a sale item? Then check what the same unit cost last month.

We’re not printing a Black Friday price in this guide. Whatever Jaspr runs this season, the same questions apply to it. The standing price is $1,099 today . Judge any offer by the five-year number, not the percentage.
