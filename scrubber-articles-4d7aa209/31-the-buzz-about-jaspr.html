The buzz about Jaspr: lab testing, reviews and what owners notice

Buzz is cheap. So what can you actually check before you buy?

You’ve seen the page. A row of gold stars, one glowing quote, a badge in the corner. It reads like proof, but you can’t open it, sort it or test it. And you’re about to spend over a thousand dollars on the strength of it.

This page works the other way. A Jaspr Air Scrubber costs $1,099 , with free shipping and a 30-day buy-back at full price. Before you spend that, here’s what’s been tested, what hasn’t, and what the people who weren’t impressed said: the lab report, the owner reviews, an independent reviewer’s verdict, and the filter and warranty terms in plain words.

The lab test, with every condition attached

On February 26, 2025, LMS Technologies, an independent microbiology lab in Bloomington, Minnesota, tested a Jaspr against airborne mold. The lab released Aspergillus niger, a common mold, into a 4,000 cubic foot chamber at 71°F and 45% humidity, and sampled the air every 5 minutes. It compared the result with a run with the device off, so natural settling and die-off are subtracted from the numbers.

Corrected removal of airborne mold spores:

  • 15.5% at 5 minutes
  • 69.9% at 30 minutes
  • 91.7% at 45 minutes
  • 97.1% at 60 minutes
  • 99.7% at 90 minutes
  • 99.8% at 120 minutes
  • No colonies recovered from 3 hours on

"Corrected" matters. Mold spores settle and die off on their own, even in an empty chamber, which is why the lab ran the chamber with the device off and subtracted that run. The figures show what the device added, not what the room did by itself.

What the test doesn’t tell you

It’s one mold species in one lab chamber. The report doesn’t say which fan speed the unit ran at . And it measures spores in the air, not mold growing on a wall. A lab chamber isn’t your living room; results depend on the room, the mold and how it’s measured.

Air purifiers are not a substitute for mold remediation. If something is growing in your home, fix the moisture and have it removed first. A Jaspr doesn’t replace that.

Reviews: a lot of them, and not all five stars

More than 1,100 owners have reviewed Jaspr on jaspr.co . Many write about buying a second, third or fourth unit for another room. Grace S. (January 20, 2026) wrote, "It immediately picks up on any change in the room - smoke, humidity, etc."

Not every review agrees. Adrian C. (February 17, 2026) gave three stars and wrote that it "doesn’t consistently respond to cooking odors, often remaining green and on low speed." Most owners describe the opposite, a fan that ramps up while they cook. We don’t know why his unit behaved differently , and we’d want him to contact support. If cooking smells are your main reason to buy, read the reviews that mention cooking before you decide.

The most common reason for a low rating isn’t airflow or noise. It’s the screen . Several low-rated reviews say the unit read Excellent from the first minute, and the owner couldn’t tell what it was doing. We understand that. A screen that reads Excellent is telling you what its sensor sees at that moment. It isn’t proof, and we don’t offer it as proof. That’s why the lab report and the filter come first on this page.

The filter and the warranty, in writing

Jaspr’s filter is one cartridge: a pre-filter, a particle filter and 1 lb of carbon. On the subscription it’s $199 every six months, charged when each one ships, or $299 for a single replacement. That’s $398 a year, about $1.09 a day, or $598 if you buy them one at a time. It costs more to run than many rivals, whose filters come to roughly $50 to $350 a year . We’d rather say so here than have you find it in a spreadsheet.

What the $199 includes: a full cartridge, free shipping, reminders before each shipment, and pause or cancel any time. While you’re enrolled and replacing the filter every six months, the warranty is for life, with free shipping both ways and a replacement unit. The lifetime upgrade depends on keeping to that schedule .

Where the name comes from

Jaspr’s founder, Michael "Mike" Feldstein, is a former disaster-restoration contractor. After Fort McMurray in 2016 he restored about 150 homes, and he watched consumer purifiers fail on job sites . "Air scrubber" is the restoration industry’s term for the machines crews run on those jobs, which is why Jaspr uses it.

Press, awards and podcasts

Mike has been a guest on a number of podcasts, and several reviewers say that’s how they first heard of Jaspr.
