A five-minute walk-through

NO EQUIPMENT NEEDED

You need a flashlight and a few minutes. Check under sinks, around window frames and caulk lines, on basement walls, and behind furniture on outside walls.

✅ A musty or earthy smell, even one that comes and goes

✅ Water stains, peeling paint, or warped drywall

✅ Condensation that doesn’t dry, or a past leak

What the CDC and EPA actually say.

- The CDC doesn’t recommend mold testing, and the EPA says sampling is usually unnecessary when growth is visible.

- Whatever the type, the advice is the same: clean up the mold and fix the moisture.

- Mold can hide behind drywall and under carpet, so a musty smell is worth taking seriously even when nothing is visible.

Before and after the crew. Never instead of it.

  • Professional crews run an air scrubber during mold work to catch the spores that get stirred up. Jaspr uses the same basic idea, recirculating filtration, built by an air quality and restoration expert for a home.
  • Jaspr works on the air: it keeps catching airborne mold spores in the room, around the clock, before the crew arrives and after they leave. Its sensors read fine particles and VOCs, not mold, so a green display isn’t a mold test.
  • Remediation works on the source: it removes the growth and fixes the moisture. Air purifiers are not a substitute for it, which is why the two work best together.

What an independent lab test showed

In a lab test, a Jaspr cut airborne mold spores by 97.1% in 1 hour and 99.8% in 2 hours. By 3 hours the lab couldn’t recover any.

The test: Aspergillus niger, a common mold, released into a 4,000 cubic foot chamber at 71°F and 45% humidity, sampled every 5 minutes and compared against a run with the purifier off, so the numbers are corrected for natural decay. LMS Technologies, an independent microbiology lab, February 26, 2025. A lab chamber isn’t your living room; results depend on the room, the mold and how it’s measured.

What this means for you.

You don’t need a test to know your next step. If you can see or smell mold, clean it up, fix the moisture that let it grow, and bring in a professional when it’s large or hidden.
